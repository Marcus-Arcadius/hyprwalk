## What hypr3d does with windows today (read these first)

- **Panels:** [src/panels.hpp](src/panels.hpp), and `collectPanels()` at [src/panels.cpp:195](src/panels.cpp#L195).
  - Every window, layer surface and xdg popup is a panel, with its subsurfaces.
  - The textures are the clients' own buffers, taken straight from `m_current.texture`, so there's no copy.
  - Panels are collected each frame at [src/main.cpp:1576](src/main.cpp#L1576). Windows placed in the world stay included even when their workspace is hidden. The plugin sends them frame callbacks itself, since Hyprland suspends hidden ones ([src/main.cpp:1726](src/main.cpp#L1726)).
- **The mouse:**
  - It always turns the camera: the `CInputManager::onMouseMoved` hook, then `onRelativeMotion` at [src/main.cpp:2147](src/main.cpp#L2147).
  - `aim()` ([:2058](src/main.cpp#L2058)) finds the window under the crosshair. `updatePointer()` ([:2104](src/main.cpp#L2104)) gives that window pointer focus, and absolute motion to the surface-local point where the crosshair lands.
  - Buttons: `onButton` ([:2280](src/main.cpp#L2280)).
  - The wheel: `onAxis` ([:2346](src/main.cpp#L2346)) sends exactly what Hyprland would, with the scroll factors and the touchpad's frames.
  - The window never gets relative motion. Pointer constraints (lock or confine) never engage in 3D, because the plugin eats `onMouseMoved`, where Hyprland applies them.
- **Keys:**
  - E or Enter starts "typing" (`setTyping`, [:1401](src/main.cpp#L1401)): the window gets keyboard focus and every key, Esc included. Super+Esc goes back to walking ([:2198](src/main.cpp#L2198)).
  - Keys held with Super or Ctrl+Alt always go to Hyprland.
  - The mouse still turns the camera while typing.
- **The cursor:** hidden in 3D (the `ensureCursorRenderingMode` hook, [:556](src/main.cpp#L556)). The renderer draws a crosshair instead ([src/renderer.cpp:1653](src/renderer.cpp#L1653)). The app's own cursor (a text I-beam, a resize arrow, a game hiding its cursor) is never shown.
- **Carrying:**
  - G picks up the window under the crosshair; G or a click puts it down; the wheel moves it nearer or further; Ctrl+wheel scales it. X sends it back to the wall.
  - `grab()` [:1836](src/main.cpp#L1836), `place()` [:1886](src/main.cpp#L1886), `returnToWall()` [:1907](src/main.cpp#L1907), and `SPlacement` [:165](src/main.cpp#L165).
  - Placements live in memory only.
  - hyprctl has `grab`, `place`, `hold`, `windows` and `reset-windows`.
- **New windows** open where Hyprland lays them out, on the wall. Nothing in the plugin reacts to a window opening.
- **The Action Menu** (Tab) has pages for emotes, expressions, gestures, the outfit and options: [src/menu.hpp](src/menu.hpp), `actionPage()` [:142](src/menu.hpp#L142), [src/control.cpp](src/control.cpp).
- **Untested:** X11 apps through XWayland (the VM has no X11 client), drag and drop, fullscreen requests in 3D, screen sharing in 3D, input methods, and any real browser, Electron app, OBS or game.

## What WaylandCraft does (the reference)

WaylandCraft is a Fabric mod: a Wayland compositor inside Minecraft, built on Smithay.
- https://github.com/EVV1E/waylandcraft (GPLv3)
- A Bedrock port: https://github.com/sfdgdrfsrf/WaylandCraft-BE (GPLv3)

It has:
- world-space windows, placed where the camera looks, attachable to walls and snapped
- V: an app launcher built from the XDG desktop entries
- G: keyboard capture
- Alt+Q: "hard" capture, which forwards Esc too and gives 3D games relative mouse motion
- B: a window manager screen
- pinning a video window to the HUD
- drag and drop between windows
- popups
- the app's cursor shape, drawn in the world
- X11 apps through xwayland-satellite

It's GPLv3 and this repo isn't: read its docs and behaviour, but copy none of its code, and say so in THIRD_PARTY.md if you describe it there. Hyprland already has XWayland built in, so you don't need xwayland-satellite.

## The tasks, in order

1. **Play mode: games inside the 3D world.** This is WaylandCraft's hard capture, done properly.
   - **What it does:** aim at a window and press a key (pick one that's free in README's Controls table; P is free). The game then gets everything:
     - keyboard focus and every key, Esc included
     - every button and the wheel
     - the mouse as the game wants it: while the game holds a pointer lock (`zwp_locked_pointer_v1`), only relative motion (`PROTO::relativePointer->sendRelativeMotion`, `protocols/RelativePointer.hpp`); with a confine, the pointer kept in its region; with neither, a pointer that moves over the panel as over a monitor
   - Pointer constraints: see `protocols/PointerConstraints.hpp` and `CInputManager::m_constraints`, and how Hyprland's own `CInputManager::onMouseMoved` applies them (the 0.55.2 source is below).
   - **While playing:**
     - The camera and your walking stop.
     - The crosshair and HUD hide.
     - The game should fill most of the view: move the camera to face the panel, or bring the panel to you. Pick the nicer one, and put it back when play mode ends.
   - **Getting out:** Super+Esc always ends play mode, as it ends typing. So do leaving 3D and the session locking. Play mode must never trap me.
   - **Controllers:** games read them themselves (SDL and evdev), and SDL drops controller events when the game has no keyboard focus. Check that a gamepad works in play mode. Say whether it works outside play mode too.
   - **Everything a game needs from the compositor:**
     - the xdg_toplevel "activated" state
     - fullscreen and maximized requests
     - `wl_output` enter and the monitor's scale
     - frame callbacks and presentation feedback at the monitor's rate
     - idle inhibit, so a video or a game keeps the screen from blanking even when its workspace is hidden
     - games that throttle on FIFO, mailbox or commit-timing

## Environment and rules

- **Python:** the nix python3 can't import numpy. Run numpy and bpy scripts as `blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 -P script.py -- args`.
- **Shell (zsh):**
  - Quote words that start with `=` (even `echo ====`), and quote globs.
  - Use `command ls`.
  - An unquoted `$v` isn't split into words: use `bash -c` or arrays.
  - The cwd resets after every command.
  - `pgrep -f` and `pkill -f` match the shell running them when the pattern is in its own command line: write `name[.]sh`.
  - `results.txt` lines can contain "N passed, M failed" from the live check, so wait for the summary with `grep -E '^[0-9]+ passed'`.
  - In build logs grep `error:`: plain `error` matches libxcb-errors on every command line.
